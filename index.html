I am an AI Engineer at Radiant Logistics, where I build agentic systems that take messy, human work data like customs documents, quote requests buried in email threads, ad-hoc analytics questions. I am passionate about building AI systems that can handle both real-world complexity and the human trust that comes with it.

I am genuinely obsessed with this class of problem. The part that gets me is the moment a system stops needing me: when an agent takes a vague, half-specified request, works out what it actually needs, goes and gets it, and comes back finished. Every agent I build is an argument that more of the work people grind through by hand can be handed over safely, if you are willing to do the boring engineering that makes it trustworthy.

Before this I spent six months at the University of Michigan's IDEA Lab researching how well language models actually understand accessibility, and five years at Cisco building the RAG and conversational infrastructure behind BotLite, a chatbot framework serving thousands of enterprise users. Accessibility research permanently changed how I evaluate anything: you stop asking whether a system works and start asking who it works for.

I hold master's degrees from the University of Washington and the Indian Institute of Science. I care about the unglamorous parts of applied AI: evaluation that means something, retrieval that actually retrieves, and interfaces people trust enough to use twice.
